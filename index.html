Research question

How can AI help designers turn words and emotions into typography and color combinations without taking away their creativity?

Moodtype Studio explores how AI can suggest several ways to style a word. Designers can compare the results, adjust the type and palette, and decide which direction feels right.

Sources & notes

How the research shaped the prototype.

Typography changes the tone

In my “happy” studies, changes in typeface, weight, and spacing gave the same word different personalities. I checked the available styles in Cooper Std, Futura PT, and Acumin Pro. Moodtype should offer several options and show the settings each typeface supports.

  • Adobe Express · Font psychology
  • Adobe Fonts · Typeface library
  • Google Design · Choosing a web font

Color depends on context

My visual study compared bright, rounded treatments for “happy” with quieter colors and open spacing for “calm,” and stronger colors and heavier type for “bold.” Culture and context can change those associations. Moodtype should show the type and palette together so users can judge the mood for themselves.

  • Cosmos · Visual inspiration
  • Adobe · Guide to color meaning
  • Verywell Mind · Color psychology overview

Contrast makes a design readable

In my contrast study, yellow on white was harder to read than dark blue on white. I want the prototype to flag low contrast and suggest alternatives people can choose to apply.

  • UC Berkeley · Color contrast
  • W3C · Minimum contrast guidance

Notes & experiment log

From the first pitch to the “Dreamy” mockup.

  1. Oct 29, 2026

    Territory pitch

    Explored a type generator, a palette explorer, and a word explorer. Asked where AI suggestions should end and the designer should take over.

  2. Nov 3, 2026

    AI brainstorming

    Refined broad project ideas into a word-to-design tool. The first suggestions felt generic, and follow-up prompts helped narrow the direction.

  3. Nov 5, 2026

    Typography studies

    Compared different treatments of “happy” and checked the actual font styles in Adobe Fonts. Identified weight and letter spacing as useful controls.

  4. Nov 13, 2026

    Color contrast comparison

    Compared text and background pairings. Planned a contrast check with suggestions the designer could choose to apply.

  5. Nov 17, 2026

    Visual mood study

    Compared “happy,” “calm,” and “bold” through type, color, and composition. Decided to preview combinations together.

  6. Nov 19, 2026

    “Dreamy” sketch and mockup

    Shared a wireframe and digital mockup showing how word exploration, three mood previews, and design controls fit together.
